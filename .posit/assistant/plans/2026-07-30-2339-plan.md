**Real bug found (Q4):** `glimpse()`/`str()` on the current data exposes mojibake in the
`zone` column, e.g. `"AmÃ©riques"` (double-encoded UTF-8). This is **not** something the
non-ASCII prevention v2 hook was ever meant to catch -- that hook only scans `*.R`,
`*.Rmd`, `*.Rd`, `DESCRIPTION`, `NAMESPACE` and explicitly **excludes**
`inst/extdata/`, `data-raw/`, and `data/` (see
`.posit/assistant/docs/2026-07-30-non-ascii-prevention-v2.md`). The actual root cause:
`R/standardize.R`'s diacritic-removal step (`consolidate_and_standardize_countries()`,
step 3) only cleans the `country_en` column via a handful of explicit `stringr::str_replace_all()`
calls -- it never touches `zone`, whose encoding is separately detected per-file in
`R/clean.R` via `readr::guess_encoding()` and can still be wrong for some year files.

Proposed fix (part of this plan, confirm before I proceed):
- Extend Step 3 of `consolidate_and_standardize_countries()` to also apply
  ASCII-normalization to `zone` (either add explicit replacements for the known zone
  strings, mirroring the `country_en` approach, or use a more general transliteration
  such as `stringi::stri_trans_general(x, "Latin-ASCII")` to avoid hardcoding every
  variant -- I'd lean toward the general approach here since `zone` has few distinct
  values and future RSF exports could introduce new mojibake variants).
- Re-run `standardize_rwb_countries()` to regenerate
  `data/processed/rwb_standardized.rds`, then re-run `data-raw/rwb_standardized.R` to
  regenerate `data/rwb_standardized.rda` (the one users actually load).
- Re-render both vignettes afterward so the displayed `glimpse()` output is clean.

If you'd rather not touch the data pipeline in this pass, the alternative is to just
document the known issue in the FAQ and move on -- let me know which you prefer.

**6. Track a Specific Country -- charts?**
Yes for the score; reconsidering ggbump for a single country's rank. `ggbump`'s bump
charts are designed to show **rank crossings among multiple entities** -- for a single
country's rank over time, a simple `ggplot2` line (with a reversed y-axis, since
rank 1 = best) communicates the same information without a new-to-this-use-case
dependency doing double duty. Proposed:
- Score: `ggplot2` line + point chart, `year_n` on x, `score` on y.
- Rank: `ggplot2` line + point chart, `year_n` on x, `rank` on y with
  `scale_y_reverse()` (rank 1 at top).
- Reserve `ggbump` for Q7, where multiple countries' ranks genuinely cross over time.
